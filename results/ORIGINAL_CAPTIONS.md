# Figure captions: chapter 4 (as supplied in revised thesis)

## Figure 4-1

Fig. 4-1 | Conceptual overview of intracellular redistribution and organelle-proximal localisation of biomimetic UCNPs. Schematic illustration of the proposed spatiotemporal framework describing the intracellular distribution behaviour of biomimetic upconversion nanoparticles (UCNPs) following cellular uptake. After internalisation, UCNPs undergo an initial endosomal/lysosomal-associated phase, followed by progressive redistribution within the cytoplasmic environment. Surface engineering and membrane camouflage modify nanoparticle–cell interactions and influence subsequent intracellular localisation patterns. Following redistribution, UCNPs exhibit proximity-associated spatial relationships with multiple intracellular organelles, including mitochondria, the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), and the Golgi apparatus. This schematic summarises the experimental framework used to quantitatively evaluate whether observed UCNP–organelle proximity exceeds that expected from random spatial distribution.

## Figure 4-6

Figure 4-6 | Non-random, time-dependent UCNP–organelle spatial proximity revealed by spatial randomisation analysis. (A) Schematic illustration of the quantitative framework used to evaluate UCNP spatial relationships with intracellular organelles following cellular uptake. After an initial lysosome-associated phase, UCNPs undergo progressive intracellular redistribution and establish measurable proximity relationships with mitochondria, ER, and Golgi structures. The schematic highlights spatial proximity analysis rather than assuming specific intracellular transport mechanisms. (B) Time-resolved proximity probability of UCNPs to mitochondria (red), ER (purple), and Golgi apparatus (gold), defined as the fraction of UCNP localisations within 100 nm of the corresponding organelle boundary. Data are presented as mean ± SEM across independent cells. (C–E) Comparison of experimentally observed UCNP–organelle proximity probabilities (coloured curves) with spatially randomised controls (black curves) for mitochondria (C), ER (D), and Golgi apparatus (E). Randomised controls were generated using Monte Carlo spatial randomisation while preserving organelle mask geometry and UCNP object density. For each condition, repeated randomisations generated a null distribution of proximity probabilities. The black line represents the mean random expectation, and shaded regions indicate the 95% confidence interval. Statistical significance was evaluated using a one-sided Monte Carlo randomisation test comparing experimental proximity probabilities with the corresponding null distributions. Asterisks indicate statistical significance (*p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001).
